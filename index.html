Mental Math Shortcuts

× 25 trick: divide the other number by 4, then multiply by 100. Ex: 32 × 25 → 32/4 = 8 → 800.
× 11 (2-digit) trick: for AB × 11, write A then (A+B) then B. Ex: 23 × 11 → 2(2+3)3 = 253. If A+B ≥ 10, carry over.
Squaring numbers ending in 5: take the digit before 5, multiply by (digit + 1), append "25". Ex: 35² → 3 × 4 = 12 → 1225. 65² → 6 × 7 = 42 → 4225.
× 5 trick: divide by 2 and multiply by 10. Ex: 86 × 5 → 43 × 10 = 430.
× 9 trick: multiply by 10 and subtract the original. Ex: 17 × 9 → 170 − 17 = 153.

Percent Formulas

Percent change: (New − Old) / Old × 100
Percent of: X% of N = (X/100) × N
Successive % changes: +X% then +Y% → multiply by (1+X/100)(1+Y/100). Ex: +20% then −20% ≠ 0% — it's 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96 → −4%.
"What % is A of B?" → A/B × 100

Top Quant Tactics

Plug in numbers for variables (try 2, 3, 5, 10, and a fraction like 1/2). Especially useful for Quantitative Comparison and "must be true" questions.
Back-solve from answer choices — start with choice C (middle value). If too high, try B/A; too low, try D/E. Saves time on word problems.
For QC: pick smart numbers. Test with 0, 1, −1, fractions (½), and large numbers. If results differ → answer is D (cannot be determined).
Estimate aggressively. Many GRE answers differ by 20%+. Round and approximate before committing to long arithmetic.
Two-pass strategy: Pass 1 — solve all in <90 sec. Pass 2 — return to flagged. NEVER let one question eat 3+ minutes.
Section-adaptive: First 5 questions of each section weight more. Spend extra 10–15 sec confirming each.
Watch for "must" vs "could": "Must be true" → find a counter-example to eliminate. "Could be true" → only need one valid case.
Units conversion: always check feet/inches, hours/minutes, $/cents before answering rate problems.

RC Question Types — Attack Patterns

Main Idea: ignore details, focus on what the author is TRYING to argue. The answer is broad enough to cover the whole passage.
Detail / Specific: the answer is in the passage. Find the line, then match. Beware paraphrased traps that change scope.
Inference: what MUST be true based on the passage (not what could be). Eliminate anything that requires outside assumption.
Function / Purpose: "Why does the author mention X?" — answer relates to the passage's argument structure, not the literal content.
Strengthen / Weaken: identify the conclusion + reasoning gap. Right answer attacks (or supports) that specific gap.
Vocab-in-context: the answer is rarely the dictionary definition. Use the sentence's tone to predict.

TC / SE Strategy

Predict before peeking. Read the sentence, write down (mentally) the word that should fill the blank. Then match.
Find the pivot: "however," "although," "despite" → the blank contrasts. "Because," "thus," "moreover" → the blank continues.
SE (Sentence Equivalence): 2 of 6 options must produce sentences with the same meaning. Eliminate any word without a near-synonym in the choices.
3-blank TC: solve the easiest blank first (often the last one). It anchors the others.
Beware: extreme language. "Always," "never," "all," "none" in answer choices are usually traps unless the passage uses those exact words.

Time Management on Verbal

Section pacing: Verbal section is 27 questions in 41 min ≈ 90 sec/question. RC passages take longer — budget 2.5 min for short, 4 min for long passages. Move fast through TC/SE to bank time for RC.
Don't re-read: on RC, scan the passage once for structure, then refer back ONLY when answering specific-detail questions. Re-reading is the #1 time killer.
